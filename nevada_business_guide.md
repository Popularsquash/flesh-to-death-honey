## Why Upgrade to an LLC?

As a sole proprietor, you and your business are legally the same entity. This means if the business is sued, your personal assets—your home, car, and savings—are at risk. An LLC creates a legal shield.

| Feature | Sole Proprietorship | Limited Liability Company (LLC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liability** | **Unlimited personal liability.** | **Limited liability.** Personal assets are protected. |
| **Legal Structure** | You and the business are one. | Separate legal entity. |
| **Credibility** | Less formal. | More professional; preferred by banks and partners. |
| **Cost & Complexity** | Simple, no state filing fees to start. | Higher initial cost (~$425) and annual fees (~$350). |

**Recommendation:** Upgrading to an LLC is the single most important step you can take to protect yourself and professionalize your business. It is a foundational requirement for long-term growth and risk management.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Registering Your Nevada LLC

Nevada has a centralized online portal called **SilverFlume** that makes this process straightforward. You will perform most of these steps at [www.nvsilverflume.gov](https://www.nvsilverflume.gov/).

**Estimated Timeline:** 1-2 weeks

### Step 1: Choose and Reserve Your Business Name

Your LLC name must be unique and not confusable with any other registered Nevada business. It must end with an LLC designator, such as "LLC," "L.L.C.," or "Limited Liability Company."

- **Action:** Search for name availability on the Nevada Secretary of State website.
- **Recommendation:** "Flesh to Death Honey Co. LLC" is a strong, clear choice.
- **Cost:** $25 to reserve a name for 90 days (optional, but recommended).

### Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Nevada requires every LLC to have a registered agent with a physical address in the state. This agent is responsible for receiving official legal and state documents on behalf of the business.

- **Action:** You can act as your own registered agent if you have a physical address in Nevada (not a P.O. Box) and are available during business hours. Alternatively, you can hire a commercial registered agent service.
- **Cost:** $0 if you do it yourself; ~$125/year for a commercial service.

### Step 3: File Articles of Organization

This is the official document that creates your LLC. It is filed with the Nevada Secretary of State through the SilverFlume portal.

- **Action:** Complete the Articles of Organization form online. You will need to provide the LLC's name, the registered agent's information, and the names of the managers or managing members (in your case, just you).
- **Cost:** $75

### Step 4: File Initial List and State Business License

Within 30 days of filing your Articles of Organization, you must file two more items:

1.  **Initial List of Managers/Managing Members:** Confirms who is running the company.
2.  **State Business License Application:** This is required for ALL businesses operating in Nevada, regardless of their structure.

- **Action:** File both of these documents together through the SilverFlume portal.
- **Cost:** $150 (Initial List) + $200 (State Business License) = $350

### Step 5: Obtain a Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)

An EIN is like a Social Security number for your business. It's required for opening a business bank account, hiring employees, and filing federal taxes. 

- **Action:** Apply for a free EIN directly from the IRS website. The process is online and takes just a few minutes.
- **Cost:** $0

### Step 6: Create an Operating Agreement

While not legally required for a single-member LLC in Nevada, this internal document is highly recommended. It outlines how the business will be run, how decisions are made, and what happens in various scenarios. It's your company's internal rulebook.

- **Action:** Draft a simple operating agreement. You can find many free templates online.
- **Cost:** $0

## Special Requirements for Your Products

Because you are selling food products and tangible goods, there are two additional registrations you must complete.

### 1. Honey Sales: Producer Certificate

In Nevada, honey is **not** considered a "cottage food." This means you cannot produce and sell it from your home kitchen without specific licensing. 

- **Action:** You must obtain a **Producer Certificate** from the **Nevada Department of Agriculture**. This involves ensuring your honey is pure and properly labeled. Contact the Department of Agriculture for the specific application and inspection process.

### 2. Sales Tax Permit

Since you are selling tangible goods (honey, apparel, beeswax products), you must collect sales tax from your Nevada customers.

- **Action:** Register for a sales tax permit with the **Nevada Department of Taxation**. This can be done online via the **My Nevada Tax** portal.
- **Cost:** $0
