## Section 1: The Business of Bees - Regulations, Sourcing, and Setup

This section covers the essential business and regulatory framework for launching your product line in Nevada.

### Nevada Cosmetic Regulations

Navigating the legal landscape is a critical first step. My research indicates a significant recent change in Nevada law that will benefit cottage industries, but with a delayed implementation. [1] [2]

**Current Regulations (Effective until July 1, 2027):**

Until the new law takes full effect, you are required to operate under the existing, more stringent regulations for cosmetic manufacturers in Nevada. This involves:

*   **Registration:** You must register your business as a cosmetic manufacturing facility with the Nevada Division of Public and Behavioral Health (DPBH).
*   **Permit:** This requires obtaining a "Permit to Operate" and a supplemental permit for cosmetic manufacturing.
*   **Inspection:** A mandatory inspection of your manufacturing facility (your home kitchen or workshop) is required. The space must adhere to Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) to prevent product adulteration.
*   **Fees:** The annual fee is approximately $300.

**The New Law: Assembly Bill 352 (Effective July 1, 2027):**

This new legislation creates a much more favorable environment for small-scale producers by establishing a "cottage cosmetics" category. [1] [2]

*   **Definition:** A "cottage cosmetic maker" is a person who manufactures cosmetics in their home with gross annual sales under $100,000.
*   **Simplified Permit:** The process will shift to a simpler permit application through the State Department of Agriculture.
*   **Allowed Products:** The law explicitly allows for the production of bath preparations, fragrances, non-coloring hair preparations, personal cleanliness items (like soap), and skin care preparations (lotions, salves, balms).
*   **Restrictions:** You cannot produce products intended for use around the eye, for injection, for internal use, or products designed to alter appearance for more than 24 hours.

**Key Takeaway:** For immediate production, you must comply with the current, more formal registration process with the DPBH. However, the future is bright for simplified, home-based production.

### FDA Regulations & Labeling

All cosmetics sold in the United States, regardless of production scale, must comply with regulations from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA) has introduced new requirements, but small businesses often have exemptions. [3] [4]

*   **Small Business Exemption:** Businesses with less than $1 million in average gross annual sales for the previous 3-year period are exempt from the most burdensome MoCRA requirements like facility registration and product listing with the FDA. **You will likely fall into this category.**
*   **Labeling is Mandatory:** You are **not** exempt from proper labeling. Every product must have a label that includes:
    1.  **Product Identity:** The common name of the product (e.g., "Leather Balm").
    2.  **Net Quantity:** The weight or volume of the product (e.g., "Net Wt. 2 oz / 57 g").
    3.  **Ingredient Declaration:** A list of all ingredients in descending order of predominance. Use the common or usual name of each ingredient.
    4.  **Manufacturer's Information:** Your business name and address (a P.O. box is not sufficient; a physical address is required).
*   **Drug Claims:** Be extremely careful with your product descriptions. Making claims that a product can treat, cure, or prevent a disease or condition (e.g., "cures eczema," "heals wounds") classifies it as a drug. Drugs require extensive and expensive FDA approval. **Avoid making medical claims.**
*   **SPF Products:** Any product claiming to provide sun protection (SPF) is regulated as a drug. The recipes in this guide that mention SPF use ingredients known to have sun-protective qualities, but you **cannot legally make an SPF claim** on your label without undergoing FDA drug testing and approval. [5]
*   **Soap Exception:** A product is only considered "soap" by the FDA if it is made primarily of alkali salts of fatty acids and makes no claims other than cleaning. If you add other ingredients for moisturizing or deodorizing and make those claims, it becomes a cosmetic.

## Section 2: The Recipes

### 1. Firestarter Cones (Combustion Cones)

These beeswax-dipped pinecones are a simple, high-margin product that is both beautiful and functional. They are perfect for selling at farmers' markets and local craft fairs.

**On-Brand Product Name Suggestions:**
*   Hellfire Starters
*   Kindling Cones
*   Desert Embers
*   Combustion Cones

**What Makes It UNIQUE:**
Unlike generic fire starters, these can be customized with unique scents, colors, and herbal additions that align with the Flesh to Death Honey Co. brand. The use of local pinecones adds a story of place and sustainability.

#### **Recipe & Formulation**

**Yields:** Approx. 12-15 medium-sized fire starters
**Prep time:** 30 minutes (plus pinecone prep)

**Ingredients:**
*   12-15 Dry, Clean Pinecones
*   1 lb (454g) Beeswax (your own or sourced)
*   **Optional Add-ins:**
    *   **Color:** 1-2 Crayons (non-toxic, paper removed) or a small amount of candle dye per pound of wax.
    *   **Scent:** 1 oz (28g) Essential Oils (Cedarwood, Pine, Cinnamon, Clove)
    *   **Herbs:** Dried Rosemary, Cedar shavings, or Cinnamon sticks to embed in the wax.

**Equipment:**
*   Dedicated Double Boiler or a pot with a metal pouring pot inside
*   Tongs
*   Parchment Paper or Wax Paper
*   Infrared Thermometer (optional but recommended)
*   Cotton Wicking (optional, for easier lighting)

#### **Step-by-Step Instructions**

1.  **Prepare Pinecones:** Ensure your pinecones are completely dry and free of dirt and bugs. If they are fresh, you can bake them on a foil-lined baking sheet at 200°F (93°C) for about an hour to dry them out and kill any pests. Let them cool completely.
2.  **Add Wicks (Optional but Recommended):** Cut 3-4 inch lengths of cotton wick. Wrap the base of the wick around the bottom scales of the pinecone, leaving about 1-2 inches sticking straight up from the top. This creates an easy-to-light fuse.
3.  **Melt Wax:** In a double boiler, slowly melt the beeswax over medium-low heat. The ideal temperature for dipping is between **150-175°F (65-80°C)**. [7] If the wax is too hot, it will be too thin and won't coat the pinecone properly. If it's too cool, it will be clumpy.
4.  **Add Scent & Color:** Once the wax is fully melted, remove it from the heat. If using, add your crayon pieces or candle dye and stir until completely dissolved. Then, add your essential oils and stir gently to combine.
5.  **Dip the Cones:** Using tongs, hold a pinecone by its top and dip it into the melted wax. Turn it to ensure it's fully coated. Lift it out and let the excess wax drip back into the pot.
6.  **Add Herbs (Optional):** As you pull the cone from the wax, you can sprinkle it with dried herbs or place larger pieces like cinnamon sticks into the crevices while the wax is still molten.
7.  **Dry and Cure:** Place the wax-dipped pinecone on parchment paper to cool and harden. This can take 30-60 minutes at room temperature. For a thicker coating, you can re-dip the cones after the first layer has cooled for about 15-20 minutes.
8.  **Packaging:** Once completely cool, they are ready to be packaged. Ideas include:
    *   Bundles of 3-4 tied with rustic twine or leather cord.
    *   Packed in a branded burlap or canvas drawstring bag.
    *   Arranged in a small wooden crate or berry basket with a branded tag.

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite. They do not expire.

**Safety/Regulatory Notes:** This is not a cosmetic, so it is not subject to the same FDA/DPBH regulations. However, you should include a warning label: "For use in fireplaces and outdoor fire pits. Never leave a fire unattended."

The scent profile is also tailored to the brand, avoiding floral or sweet notes.

#### **Step-by-Step Instructions**

1.  **Weigh Ingredients:** Using a digital scale, accurately weigh out all your butters, waxes, and oils into your pouring pot.
2.  **Melt Gently:** Place the pouring pot in your double boiler and melt the ingredients together over low to medium heat. Stir occasionally with a spatula until everything is completely liquid and homogenous.
3.  **Cool Slightly & Add Scent:** Remove the pot from the heat. Let it cool for a minute or two. This prevents the volatile essential oils from flashing off. Add your essential oil blend and stir thoroughly.
4.  **Pour into Tins:** Carefully pour the hot mixture into your metal tins. Be careful as the mixture will be hot.
5.  **Cool and Set:** Let the tins sit undisturbed at room temperature for several hours, or until completely solid. Do not place them in the fridge, as this can cause cracking or a grainy texture.
6.  **Label and Package:** Once set, cap the tins and apply your branding and legally compliant labels.

### 4. Charcoal Soap

Activated charcoal soap is a classic for a reason. It’s detoxifying and great for oily or acne-prone skin. These recipes are designed to be rugged and on-brand, with options for both cold process and melt-and-pour methods.

**On-Brand Product Name Suggestions:**

**What Makes It UNIQUE:**
The addition of beeswax to the cold process recipe adds hardness and a silky, lotion-like quality to the lather. The scent profiles are masculine and earthy, and the use of coffee grounds provides excellent exfoliation. The motorcycle part shapes are a killer branding move.

#### **Formulations**

**Equipment:**
*   Digital Scale, Safety Goggles, Gloves
*   Stick Blender (for Cold Process)
*   Soap Molds (Motorcycle Part Molds Recommended) [10]
*   Stainless Steel Pots, Silicone Spatulas

--- 

**Formulation 1: Cold Process Pine Tar & Charcoal Soap**

This is an advanced recipe for true soap makers. It creates a hard, long-lasting, and beautifully rustic bar.

**Yields:** Approx. 3 lbs of soap

| Ingredient | Weight (grams) | Percentage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oils & Waxes** | | |
| Olive Oil | 454g | 45% |
| Coconut Oil | 252g | 25% |
| Lard or Tallow | 202g | 20% |
| Beeswax | 50g | 5% |
| Castor Oil | 50g | 5% |
| **Lye Solution** | | |
| Distilled Water | 288g | | 
| Sodium Hydroxide (Lye) | 138g | 6% Superfat |
| **Additives at Trace** | | |
| Pine Tar | 50g | |
| Activated Charcoal | 2 Tbsp | |
| Bentonite Clay | 1 Tbsp | |
| Pine & Tea Tree Essential Oils | 30g | |

**Cold Process Instructions:**
1.  **Safety First:** Put on your goggles and gloves. Weigh lye and distilled water in separate, heat-safe containers. In a well-ventilated area, slowly add the lye to the water (never the other way around), stirring until dissolved. The mixture will get very hot. Set aside to cool to 100-120°F.
2.  **Melt Oils:** Melt the lard/tallow and beeswax. Add the liquid oils.
3.  **Mix:** When both the lye-water and oils are around 100-120°F, slowly pour the lye-water into the oils. Use a stick blender to mix until it reaches "trace" (the consistency of thin pudding).
4.  **Additives:** Stir in the pine tar, charcoal, clay, and essential oils.
5.  **Pour & Cure:** Pour into your mold. Insulate with a towel for 24 hours. Unmold after 24-48 hours, cut into bars, and cure in a well-ventilated area for 4-6 weeks.

--- 

**Formulation 2: Melt-and-Pour Charcoal & Coffee Scrub**

This is a much simpler, beginner-friendly option.

| Ingredient | Amount | Purpose |

**Melt-and-Pour Instructions:**

2.  **Additives:** Once melted, remove from heat and stir in the charcoal, coffee grounds, and essential oil.
3.  **Pour:** Pour into your molds.
4.  **Cool:** Let cool until completely hard (a few hours). Unmold, and the soap is ready to use immediately.

### 5. Healing Salve (Road Rash & Tattoo Aftercare)

This is a powerful, all-purpose healing salve formulated with botanicals known for their skin-soothing and regenerative properties. It’s perfect for cuts, scrapes, road rash, and as a tattoo aftercare balm.

**On-Brand Product Name Suggestions:**
*   Road Rash Repair
*   Asphalt Salve
*   Ink Guard Tattoo Balm
*   Wound Warrior Salve

**What Makes It UNIQUE:**
This isn't just a simple balm. It starts with a slow infusion of powerful healing herbs (calendula, comfrey, plantain) into oil. This herbal oil is then combined with other potent ingredients like tea tree oil and lavender to create a truly effective healing product. [11]

#### **Formulations**

**Equipment:**
*   Digital Scale
*   Double Boiler
*   Cheesecloth or fine mesh strainer
*   Glass jar for infusion
*   1 oz or 2 oz Tins

--- 

**Step 1: Create the Infused Herbal Oil**

This is the heart of your salve. You can make a larger batch of this oil and store it for future use.

**Ingredients:**
*   1 part Dried Calendula Flowers
*   1 part Dried Comfrey Leaf
*   1 part Dried Plantain Leaf
*   10 parts Olive Oil or Sweet Almond Oil

**Instructions:**
1.  **Combine:** Place the dried herbs in a clean, dry glass jar.
2.  **Infuse:** Pour the oil over the herbs, ensuring they are completely submerged. 
3.  **Slow Method (Best):** Cap the jar and place it in a warm, sunny spot for 2-4 weeks, shaking it daily.
4.  **Quick Method:** Place the jar in a saucepan with a few inches of water. Gently heat the water (do not boil) for 4-8 hours, ensuring the water doesn’t evaporate. 
5.  **Strain:** Once infused, strain the oil through cheesecloth, squeezing out as much oil as possible from the herbs. This is your finished herbal oil.

--- 

**Step 2: Formulate the Salve**

This recipe uses your freshly made infused oil.

| Ingredient | Parts by Weight | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Infused Herbal Oil | 4 | The active healing base. |
| Beeswax | 1 | Creates the protective salve structure. |
| Shea Butter | 0.5 | Adds creaminess and extra moisture. |

### 6. Egyptian Magic-Style All-Purpose Healing Cream

This is the flagship product, a tribute to the cult-favorite Egyptian Magic cream. The original is a simple yet powerful blend of six bee products and olive oil. [12] Our version, the "Flesh to Death" edition, will be formulated in tiers, allowing for a range of price points and showcasing the incredible power of the hive.

**On-Brand Product Name Suggestions:**
*   The Six: All-Purpose Remedy
*   Hive Alchemy Healing Cream
*   Queen Bee's Gold
*   Necropolis Balm

**What Makes It UNIQUE:**
While many have tried to replicate Egyptian Magic, few do it well. Our advantage is the direct access to high-quality hive products. We will focus on the unique properties of propolis and royal jelly, creating a product that is not just a moisturizer but a true, multi-purpose healing and beauty balm. The tiered approach also makes the most potent ingredients accessible to a wider audience.

#### **Understanding the Ingredients**

*   **Olive Oil:** The carrier. A stable, nourishing oil.
*   **Beeswax:** The structurant. Creates the balm consistency and provides a protective barrier.
*   **Honey:** A natural humectant (draws moisture to the skin) and is antibacterial.
*   **Bee Pollen:** Rich in vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants. Must be ground finely.
*   **Propolis:** The "immune system" of the hive. A resinous substance bees create from tree sap. It is powerfully antibacterial, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and promotes wound healing. [13]
*   **Royal Jelly:** The food of the queen bee. An incredibly complex substance rich in proteins, vitamins (especially B vitamins), and unique fatty acids. It is believed to promote collagen production and skin renewal. [14]

#### **Sourcing Propolis & Royal Jelly**

These are premium ingredients and their quality is paramount.

*   **Propolis:** Can be harvested from your own hives using a propolis trap (a special screen placed on top of the frames). The raw propolis is then scraped off and can be used to make a tincture. Alternatively, it can be purchased raw from beekeeping suppliers like **Draper Bee** or **Mickelberry Gardens**.
*   **Royal Jelly:** Harvesting royal jelly is a very specialized and labor-intensive process that is likely beyond the scope of a small operation. It involves grafting larvae into queen cells and harvesting the jelly a few days later. It is highly perishable and must be kept frozen or refrigerated. The best approach is to source it from a reputable supplier like **Stakich** or **Beehive Botanicals**, who sell it fresh (frozen) or mixed with honey.

#### **How to Make a Propolis Tincture**

A tincture is the easiest way to incorporate propolis into your formulations.

1.  **Freeze Propolis:** Freeze your raw propolis for a few hours. This makes it brittle and easier to grind.
2.  **Grind:** Grind the frozen propolis into a coarse powder using a dedicated coffee grinder or mortar and pestle.
3.  **Combine:** Combine the propolis powder with high-proof grain alcohol (190 proof Everclear is ideal) in a glass jar. A standard ratio is **1 part propolis to 4 parts alcohol by weight**.
4.  **Infuse:** Seal the jar and store it in a dark place for 2-4 weeks, shaking it daily.
5.  **Strain:** Strain the liquid through a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth. The resulting dark, fragrant liquid is your propolis tincture.

#### **Step-by-Step Instructions (for Premium Formulation)**

This process requires careful temperature control and vigorous mixing.

1.  **Melt Waxes & Oils:** In a double boiler, gently melt the beeswax into the olive oil until completely liquid. Remove from heat.
2.  **Cool Down:** This is the most critical step. Let the oil/wax mixture cool to **approximately 100-110°F (38-43°C)**. It should be warm, but not hot. This is crucial to avoid cooking the raw honey and royal jelly, which would destroy their beneficial enzymes.
3.  **Prepare the Hive Blend:** In a separate bowl, whisk together the raw honey, fresh royal jelly, ground bee pollen, and propolis tincture until you have a smooth, homogenous paste.
4.  **Emulsify:** Slowly drizzle the hive blend into the slightly cooled oil/wax mixture while continuously mixing with an **immersion blender**. Keep blending for 1-2 minutes until the mixture becomes opaque, creamy, and fully emulsified. You are creating a water-in-oil emulsion, and the stick blender is key to making it stable.
5.  **Pour and Set:** Pour the cream into your sanitized glass jars. Let it cool and set at room temperature. The final consistency will be a soft, rich, honey-like balm.

#### **Royal Jelly Preservation**

Fresh royal jelly is too perishable to sell as-is without frozen shipping. The best way to offer it is preserved in honey.

*   **Product:** Royal Jelly Infused Honey
*   **Ratio:** 1 part Fresh Royal Jelly to 4 parts Raw Honey by weight.
*   **Instructions:**
    1.  Allow the fresh (frozen) royal jelly to thaw slightly.
    2.  In a clean bowl, gently but thoroughly fold the royal jelly into the raw honey until completely combined. Do not heat.
    3.  Package in small glass jars.
*   **Dosage/Application:** Can be taken internally as a supplement (e.g., 1/2 tsp daily) or used as a face mask.
*   **Cost Analysis:** This is a high-value product. Based on the cost of royal jelly ($3-5 per ounce) and honey, the cost for a 4 oz jar would be in the range of $8-12.
*   **Suggested Retail Price (4 oz jar):** $25.00 - $40.00.
*   **Shelf Life:** When mixed with honey, the royal jelly is preserved. It should be stored in the refrigerator and will last for up to 1 year.
